# Cinematic Video (code-rendered)

Every frame is a pure function of time `t`, rendered by a browser that you drive and encoded by ffmpeg. Nothing is generated by a video model. That gives three things AI video can't: the user's real UI pixels, frame-exact sync with sound, and re-renders that come out identical every time.

```
assets (screens, model, logo, brand) ──► scene.html  (three.js + DOM, render(t))
                                          │  scripts/render.mjs   headless Chromium → frames/00000.png…
                                          │  SCENE.cues()          ─► cues.json
                                          ▼  scripts/audio.mjs     cues.json → music.wav (beat-locked)
                                    scripts/encode.mjs  frames + wav → film.mp4 (H.264, yuv420p, faststart)
```

## Workflow

1. **Brief.** Pull the spec from the request, using `references/prompt-template.md` as a checklist: format, length, subject, assets, brand, story, platform. Look through the asset folders yourself. Open every screenshot and check the model loads before you plan anything. Ask only about things you can't infer or default: the product name, the brand colour if there's no logo to sample it from, and the tagline. Everything else has a default below.
2. **Shot list first.** Write the beat sheet as a table (shot, beats, camera move, screen, title, SFX) and check it against the rules in *Non-negotiables*. The length of every shot is a whole number of beats.
3. **Scaffold.** In a work folder (never inside the user's asset folder): `npm i three playwright ffmpeg-static @fontsource/inter`, then copy `templates/scene.html` and `scripts/`. The template is a complete, working phone product film. For a phone or app, edit its `FILM` block. For other subjects, keep the architecture (timeline → `pose(t)` → `render(t)` → `overlays(t)`) and replace the objects. See `references/craft.md#adapting`.
4. **Look-dev with stills, not full renders.** `node scripts/render.mjs --scene scene.html --out stills --ss 1 --stills "1,3,6,…"` renders one still per shot plus the moment of each cut. Build a contact sheet and **look at it** with the Read tool. Fix composition, lighting and legibility at this stage, while it's cheap.
5. **Motion check.** `--every 3 --ss 1` renders a 10 fps preview. Encode it and check that the timing feels right and that no screen swap is visible.
6. **Final.** Run a full render at `--ss 2` (the default), then `audio.mjs`, then `encode.mjs`. Verify it with `ffprobe` (duration, fps, resolution, audio stream), look at 3–4 extracted frames, and check that `render.mjs` printed no `CHECK:` warnings. Deliver the MP4 along with the poster frame and the shot list.

Budget (measured, SwiftShader, no GPU): the 30 s template film took **~28 min at `--ss 1`** (≈1.8 s/frame on average; spin frames with 12 motion-blur subframes are the slowest), and `--ss 2` costs roughly 2–3× that. Always run full renders in the background, and split them across 3–4 processes with disjoint `--from/--to` ranges. A still costs about 2–3 s.

## Non-negotiables (what makes it read as "Apple")

- **One continuous take.** Hide every change of screen content, object or camera inside a move: the phone is edge-on or facing away, a spin, a whip, or an occluding pass. Never cross-fade UI on a visible screen. The template's `check()` enforces this for phone shots.
- **Real pixels.** The user's screenshots, logo and UI are mapped as textures exactly as supplied: cover-fit, rounded corners, never redrawn, restyled, upscaled with AI or "improved". Animate *on top* of them: typing, reveals, highlights, taps.
- **One camera move per shot.** Push, orbit, rise or drift, eased, and never combined. Stillness counts as a move.
- **Everything on the beat.** Pick a BPM, which sets the beat length (100 BPM = 0.6 s). Cuts, title entrances, SFX hits and UI events all land on beats or half-beats. Time lives in beats in the config and is converted to seconds in exactly one place.
- **Pure black void, sculpted by light.** No floor, props, hands, gradient backdrops or lens flares. Materials such as titanium, glass and aluminium are lit by an environment map made of emissive light strips (warm rim behind-left, cool rim behind-right, soft top key), so the edges glow.
- **Motion blur only when something moves fast.** Average subframes across a 180° shutter. Static frames stay razor sharp.
- **Typography is restrained.** One display face (Inter Display / SF Pro Display Semibold), a white first line and a grey (#86868b) second line, with at most one key word in the brand colour. Titles come in with fade + ~40px slide-up + blur-to-sharp on an expo-out ease. Keep titles to about 22 characters per line and 2 lines at most.
- **Platform-safe layout.** Vertical 1080×1920: titles in the y 230–450 band, and nothing important below y 1550 or in the right-hand ~120px (Reels, TikTok and Shorts UI). `?guides=1` overlays these zones.
- **Loops cleanly.** Open from black, end on logo → fade to black, and fade the audio to digital silence.
- **Deterministic.** Never use `Math.random()`, `Date.now()`, `requestAnimationFrame` timing or CSS transitions. Use seeded PRNGs, compute time as `frame / fps` (never an accumulated float), wait for fonts and textures before frame 0, and use SwiftShader in headless mode.

## Default film structure (adapt the counts to the requested length)

| # | Shot | Beats @100 BPM | Move | Sound |
|---|------|------|------|-------|
| 1 | Cold open: macro along the frame edge / material detail | 4 | slow slide | riser builds |
| 2 | Hero reveal: spin from back to front, product name | 8 | push-in | whoosh → **boom** on the reveal |
| 3–5 | One feature per shot: screen + UI animation + two-line title | 8 each | orbit / rise / push (vary) | whoosh on every spin |
| 6 | Fan of 3 devices (or the family of products) | 6 | pull-back | whoosh |
| 7 | Logo + tagline + CTA ("Free on App Store / Google Play"), fade to black | 8 | none | **chime** on the logo |

That comes to 50 beats = 30.0 s. For 15 s, keep 1 feature and shorten the hero. For 60 s, use 6–8 features and add a "detail" macro shot between groups. For horizontal 16:9 (1920×1080), set the camera's fov to ~24°, keep titles in the left third, and put the object right of centre.

## Scripts

- `scripts/render.mjs`: static server + headless Chromium. It runs the page's `SCENE.render(t)` for each frame and saves PNG screenshots. Flags: `--from/--to/--every/--stills/--ss/--cues`. It prints `CHECK:` warnings from `SCENE.check()`.
- `scripts/audio.mjs`: `cues.json` → stereo 48 kHz WAV. The bed (pad chords, kick, hats) is locked to the BPM, with SFX `boom | whoosh | chime | tick | riser`. Moods: `minimal | driving | ambient`. It warns about any hit that falls off the half-beat grid.
- `scripts/encode.mjs`: frames + WAV → H.264 High, CRF 16, yuv420p, AAC 256k, `+faststart`. Uses system ffmpeg, or `ffmpeg-static` if that's missing.
- `templates/scene.html`: a complete phone product film (a procedural titanium phone, or the user's GLB via `FILM.model`) with a light-strip env map, a canvas screen texture with UI animations, beat-based shots, titles, fan and logo end card, subframe motion blur, 2× supersampling, `check()` and `cues()`. Scrub any frame in a browser with `scene.html?t=6.3&guides=1&ss=1`.

## Gotchas

- Serve over http (render.mjs does this). `file://` breaks ES modules and texture loading.
- A GLB's screen mesh must have proper 0–1 UVs. Set `FILM.screenMesh` to its name, and if the UI appears mirrored or upside-down, flip `tex.flipY` or `tex.repeat.x = -1`. FBX: convert it to GLB first (`npx fbx2gltf` or Blender) rather than loading FBX at render time.
- Hardware MSAA under SwiftShader leaves seams along triangle edges. The template supersamples (`SS=2`) instead.
- Canvas textures need `needsUpdate = true` on every frame they change, and `colorSpace = SRGBColorSpace`, or the UI looks washed out.
- Fonts: call `await document.fonts.ready` before frame 0, and ship the font files locally (CDNs may be blocked in sandboxes).
- Keep every string of UI that you *type* into the screen in the user's own words and language. Don't invent product claims. Ask for them, or use neutral placeholders and flag them.
